College Affordability Challenge

Choose a real university, investigate its published costs, research the local cost of living, and build a plan to see whether you can afford one year of college.

Mission 1 — Choose your university

Use a real institution. Start with its official Cost of Attendance page and, if available, its Net Price Calculator.

Mission 2 — Research annual expenses

Enter amounts for one academic year. Use the university's published figures where possible. For local rent and food, find a credible source for the university's area.

University and study expenses

Local cost of living

Research tip: local government or university housing information, reputable rental-market reports, and regional cost-of-living data are useful starting points. Record the source and date you accessed it.

Avoid double-counting. Many universities' Cost of Attendance estimates already include housing, meals, books, transportation, and personal expenses. Check what is included before adding local estimates. If you use your own local rent and food research, replace the comparable university estimates rather than adding both.

Mission 3 — Identify your financial resources

Estimate what you could actually use to pay for the year. Enter grants and scholarships separately from loans.

Use realistic estimates. Work earnings should be the amount remaining for college costs after taxes and personal spending—not simply total wages earned.

Mission 4 — Run the affordability test

Calculate the annual funding gap, then decide what you would do. The estimate assumes the same costs and resources each year, so actual future costs may differ.
